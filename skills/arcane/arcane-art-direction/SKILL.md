# Art direction that survives gameplay

## Establish the visual contract
Start from the actual brief and existing project. Identify the audience, player camera, target devices, visual era and intended mood. For a full game, describe how its playable spaces, characters, props, GUI and relevant animation/effects belong together. For a single prop or lighting edit, constrain the contract to that work; do not redesign the game.

Record a small set of usable decisions: silhouette families, proportion and scale relationships, palette roles, material response, typography, light hierarchy and detail density. Separate the primary landmark/action from supporting content. Use references for construction principles with source and rights recorded; recognizable protected characters or branding are not automatically licensed.

Build one representative playable composition before multiplying content: a route or room, its focal object, an interaction, and the associated interface where relevant. Choose an intentional style; blocky, painted, stylized and realistic worlds have different success criteria. Preserve coherent existing art. Do not add people or scale figures to asset reviews unless requested.

## Direct content, not just a mood
Assign each asset a role, viewing distance, footprint, material family, variation strategy and performance allowance. Distinguish hero assets from reusable modules and background forms. Richness comes from purposeful large/medium/small forms, spatial storytelling and useful variation, not a uniform layer of noise or random clutter.

Author original model geometry, UVs, textures and PBR in Blender when models are needed. Toolbox/free assets require an express user request; a general full-game or realism request does not authorize them. Use the current permitted authoring/import route and exact-model human review; a skill cannot grant capabilities or approval.

Give other disciplines concrete contracts: interaction/grip points to gameplay, navigable gaps to level design, palette and information priority to GUI, readable anticipation/impact/recovery to motion and effects. Add sound or spectacle when it communicates or supports the requested experience, not as compulsory ornament on every edit.

## Acceptance
Compare primary proportions and silhouettes before polishing maps. Review a neutral source view and a representative engine view from the real player camera, then inspect the busiest relevant gameplay state. Check focal hierarchy, material scale, recurring motifs, background competition, legibility on the smallest target screen and consistency among adjacent assets.

Use matched cameras/lighting for revisions and record the specific defect corrected. A Blender render, interactive preview, imported engine view and played scene are different evidence. Report which were actually inspected, unresolved mismatches and measured device limits. Do not replace those observations with a quality score, universal photorealism promise or claim that a written style contract proves finished art.
