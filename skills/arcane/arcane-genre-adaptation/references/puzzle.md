# Puzzle / educational challenge

The player forms a hypothesis, manipulates a consistent rule and learns from feedback. Decide what is discovered rather than merely executed. For educational work, name the actual concept being practiced and how an action demonstrates it; a quiz label does not prove learning.

## A progression of ideas

First introduce one rule, then require a new combination and a recoverable reset. A complete brief needs the requested sequence of ideas/locations/challenges and a meaningful finish or ongoing puzzle source. Increase reasoning demands through combinations, constraints and perspectives, not only more identical switches. Do not impose a fixed puzzle count or hints/leaderboards when the user asked to repair one mechanism.

Represent puzzle state, valid moves, success conditions and irreversible transitions explicitly. The server checks shared-world actions and rewards; multiple players cannot consume a key or complete a puzzle twice. Identify deadlocks and recoverability, including partially solved states, disconnect and reset while an animation is running. Accept alternative valid solutions the rules genuinely permit; do not hard-code one demonstration sequence as the only answer.

## Feedback and accessible reasoning

Use layout, symbols, object motion, sound and text to expose causes and relevant state without revealing the solution immediately. Camera framing and collision must let players inspect/manipulate the actual elements. Hints can escalate from orientation to a specific clue when appropriate, with no forced purchase. Essential rules need alternatives to color/audio alone and controls beyond precision dragging. Original thematic props support the puzzle's logic; actual authorized media cannot be replaced by named placeholders.

Observe participants explaining a hypothesis, making an informative mistake and revising it. Test every delivered puzzle, alternative solutions, unsolvable states, reset, shared interactions and requested save/resume. Separate a scenario following known steps from evidence that the puzzle is discoverable or educationally effective. Use gameplay-systems, onboarding, interface and accessibility guidance; technical completion flags do not establish satisfying discovery or learner comprehension.
