# Party / round-based competition

The player learns a rule quickly, reacts, collaborates or deceives within the actual brief, then retries with variation. A party collection needs distinct interaction verbs and useful eliminated-player flow; repainting one dodge-floor game is not a varied roster.

## Compose games into a session

Prove contrasting small games plus lobby -> instructions -> play -> result -> transition. For a full request, author the promised roster and session arc with fair rotation, joining rules and a satisfying result/replay. Avoid a fixed universal minigame count; compare each delivered game's goal, input, decision, failure and scoring to detect cosmetic duplication.

Each game declares its map/participants, start readiness, valid actions, score/win/tie rules, timeout and cleanup. A session/round epoch rejects delayed callbacks from the last game. Handle absent participants, simultaneous finishes, disconnections and no eligible winner. Waiting/spectating should be understandable and respectful of round length, not a long unskippable punishment. Award session progress once under real requested persistence if it exists.

## Fast teaching and expressive feedback

Use a short rule explanation with an actual visual demonstration/affordance, not a wall of text. Arena layout, distinctive props, character animation and sound cues explain the current task; results show why someone won. Keep essential information visible across supported devices and provide alternatives to audio/color-only cues. VFX should celebrate outcomes without carrying into the next game or hiding its instructions. All media need actual availability/permission.

Observe new participants explaining the rule and acting without coaching, then transitioning to a different game. Test ties, final simultaneous events, leaving during results, late joining and repeated full sessions. Count retained instances/connections and ensure no old input or camera ownership survives. `arcane-rounds`, onboarding, interface and gameplay-feel guidance cover shared mechanics; a working countdown and one minigame do not prove the full collection or multiplayer fun.
