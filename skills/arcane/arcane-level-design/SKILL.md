# Design playable, finished spaces

## Start with what happens here
Establish avatar/camera scale, movement and interaction reach, player count, session purpose and constraints. Map spawn→orientation→first action→main routes→objectives→failure/recovery→exit or next area. Give landmarks, alternate routes, exposure, shortcuts and resting spaces a gameplay purpose. An irregular outline or many props does not create meaningful layout.

For a full-game request, plan an appropriate collection of spaces/challenges with different decisions, pacing and visual identities. Vary route topology, elevation, hazards, encounter rhythm or rules rather than duplicating one layout with another palette. Respect a brief that intentionally uses one tightly developed location. Record each space's role, dependencies and completion test.

## Prove dimensions and flow
Use a temporary blockout to measure route times, sightlines, jump/vehicle clearance, spawn safety, camera clipping and interaction access. Keep it identified as a planning stage. Test the real player/controller, including recovery and alternate paths. PvP/chases need counterplay and spawn protection; exploration, puzzles and social spaces need their own relevant decisions instead of gratuitous choke points.

Separate coordinate systems and units, verify transforms/pivots and measure actual bounds/collision. Account for slopes, stopping distance and camera height instead of drawing a plausible aerial path. Reserve readable space around landmarks and objectives before scattering scenery. Keep invisible boundaries and streaming transitions from becoming accidental traps.

## Replace the blockout with a coherent environment
Create a composition hierarchy: primary destination, supporting landmarks, route cues and quiet space. Model and place structural/hero assets through the original Blender/UV/PBR/import workflow; do not silently finish with stock primitives or unrequested Toolbox content. Reuse a purposeful modular kit with controlled scale/material/shape variation rather than obvious repeated stamps.

Finish ground as deliberately as objects. Establish landform/ground materials, contact and transitions, grass or appropriate cover, paths, edges and undergrowth/clutter at several scales. A forest on a bare checker baseplate is incomplete. Ground cover must preserve routes and performance; dense placement counts alone are not ecological or visual quality.

Integrate lighting, atmosphere, localized sound and relevant environmental motion/VFX with navigation and mood. Avoid fog, bloom or clutter that hides collision, unmodeled surfaces or missing assets. A well/building needs believable placement, approach, interaction/collision and contact with its surroundings; it cannot be an isolated token prop in a scene checklist.

## Acceptance at player scale
Walk/drive actual routes and interact with required objects, not only inspect an overhead capture. Review approach, eye-level, landmark and difficult camera views. Check repeated sections for new decisions, foreground/midground/background balance and visible seams/floating assets. Test representative devices/player counts and budget extremes with performance guidance. Record missing imports, inaccessible spaces and unverified gameplay explicitly. Finish all requested spaces and transitions after the first route works.
