# Asset history retention

Arcane retains asset jobs without moving or deleting their files. Its ledger holds at most 4096 archived entries within 4 MiB; active history remains bounded at 256 jobs. Before generation or GLB adoption needs another active slot, the service archives up to 32 eligible older jobs. Invalid input is refused before any retention change. Full or pinned history refuses new work with guidance instead of deleting evidence.

A terminal journal alone does not establish that its artifacts are usable. Manifest availability comes from verified host evidence. A rebuilt archived success with no such fact establishes availability only when a scoped read validates the current saved exports and rechecks the journal. Downloads and import preparation retain their independent checks. An interrupted job additionally needs a currently valid worker-resolution marker bound to its exact journal. Outstanding worker reservations, live operations, read leases, preparation records, stage work and uncertain outcomes prevent archival.

Host storage queries retain preparation history across every attempt. Stage work stays pinned while running, awaiting engine completion or needing reconciliation. A closed, fully staged transfer remains pinned because there is no engine-completion release record yet. Upload intents and uncertain outcomes also retain a pin; only a valid successful receipt with a positive asset ID and matching stored intent can release it. Invalid, corrupt, busy or closed storage refuses the query instead of reporting absence. These queries neither issue engine commands nor modify retained storage.

Journal digests and filesystem fingerprints detect observed changes; they do not authenticate a same-user writer able to replace all matching records. A startup fingerprint is only a checkpoint. Full lookup and restore must validate current bounded journal and resolution bytes again. Missing or conflicting evidence must retain admission fences.

Global and captured-chat history hydrate archived UUIDs on demand. Pages contain at most 64 jobs and unavailable records within 512 KiB. A missing or conflicting record remains an explicit identity; it is not replaced with a fake completed job. Chat reads recheck the captured project, conversation and consumed cursor after filesystem awaits. The desktop keeps a bounded window of loaded records; paging never deletes server history. Read leases protect owned lookups and artifact verification, and service close joins them.

Human controls show history health and offer restoration, index rebuild and confirmation of an uncertain change. Restoring an archived UUID uses one ledger write; at capacity it swaps one eligible active job out in the same transaction. These controls never restart generation or import an asset. Native and agent credentials cannot invoke them. Trusted activation, one flight, context retirement and unknown-outcome handling prevent automatic replay after a lost response.

An explicit rebuild inspects the complete bounded inventory and rechecks current host facts. It preserves original ledger bytes, including invalid UTF-8, in a unique flushed and byte-verified backup before replacement. At most 16 backups are allowed; none is deleted to make room. Newly discovered running, malformed or missing journals stay visible and keep admission fenced. Rebuilding does not rewrite their journals. A valid prior archived record without current eligible evidence is refused. An uncertain replacement stays fenced; confirmation validates the actual current ledger and retained evidence rather than acknowledging missing work.

The durable store uses the production atomic writer, checks the canonical root and direct child identities, and revalidates file witnesses before replacement. Startup uses bounded metadata for archived jobs; it does not open their journals or artifacts. A fixture with 256 active and 4096 archived jobs measured a warm metadata scan of about 1.6 seconds on the development machine. This is a local measurement, not a general latency guarantee.

Verification covers owned filesystem and SQLite faults, capacity swaps, restart, corrupt-byte preservation, unavailable history, exact scoped pagination, HTTP/MCP ownership, cancellation and actual Edge controls. Browser/controller acceptance uses disposable failed-job records and verifies the retained bytes. Native provider inference, engine delivery and the complete installed customer workflow remain separate acceptance gates.
